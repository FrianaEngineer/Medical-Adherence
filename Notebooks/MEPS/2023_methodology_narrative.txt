Methodology Narrative: Medication Adherence from MEPS 2023

This analysis estimates chronic medication adherence for calendar year 2023 using the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ) Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (MEPS). The goal is to measure, for each person–drug pair linked to a chronic condition and a maintenance medication, the share of eligible observation days that are covered by dispensed days of supply. The resulting measure is a Proportion of Days Covered (PDC)–style adherence ratio expressed as a percentage and capped at one hundred percent. The atomic unit of analysis is one row per person and drug (DUPERSID × DRUGIDX) after chronic-condition and chronic-drug filtering, with multi-condition context retained separately so that condition-level summaries do not inflate days of supply.

Four MEPS public-use files for 2023 form the analytic backbone. The Prescribed Medicines file (h248a.xlsx) contributes one row per prescription fill and supplies days of supply (RXDAYSUP), medication identifiers (RXNAME, RXNDC, DRUGIDX), Multum therapeutic class codes (TC1, TC1S1), medication start year and month (RXBEGYRX, RXBEGMM), fill and link keys (RXRECIDX, LINKIDX), and spending fields used elsewhere in the project. The Condition–Event Link file (h248if1.xlsx, CLNK) connects prescription events to medical conditions. The Medical Conditions file (h249.xlsx) provides person-level current conditions coded to three-digit ICD-10 (ICD10CDX). The Full-Year Consolidated Person file (h251.xlsx) contributes demographics (AGE23X, SEX, RACEV2X, INSCOV23, POVCAT23, FAMINC23) and the survey participation variables (PSTATS31, PSTATS42, PSTATS53 together with BEGRF and ENDRF month and year fields by round) that define each person’s eligible window in 2023. Two curated crosswalks complete the pipeline: is_chronic.xlsx, a draft allowlist of three-digit ICD-10 codes treated as chronic, and unique_rxname_chronic_labeled_revised.csv, a hand-labeled list of unique drug names classified as chronic, flare-up, or non-chronic.

Construction begins with all prescription fills in h248a for 2023 (192,275 fills among 11,858 persons). Days of supply are retained only when RXDAYSUP falls between 1 and 989 inclusive. Values of 999 are excluded because MEPS uses that code to flag as-needed medications rather than a literal day count; treating 999 as days supplied would manufacture near-perfect adherence. Negative sentinel codes (−1, −7, −8, −9, −15), which encode missing or special statuses such as Don’t Know, are likewise dropped. This step removes 52,594 fills and 2,093 persons. Fills are then required to have a usable medication start year: RXBEGYRX must be a positive calendar year rather than a MEPS sentinel (−1, −7, −8). Rows with unknown start year are excluded because later denominator logic depends on whether the drug began before or during 2023; that filter removes a further 25,663 fills and 510 persons and leaves 114,018 fills among 9,255 persons.

Chronic conditions are identified by merging h249 onto the is_chronic allowlist and keeping only conditions with is_chronic greater than zero. Of 63,656 condition rows, 30,372 remain after this filter (10,137 persons with at least one chronic ICD on the allowlist). Prescription fills are then linked to those chronic conditions through CLNK. Only CLNK rows with EVENTYPE equal to 8 (prescribed medicine events) are used. The join keys are person identifier plus event link—DUPERSID together with LINKIDX on the fill matched to EVNTIDX on CLNK—never person alone (which would explode into a Cartesian product) and never the link index alone (which is round-scoped and unsafe across persons). After the left join, fills that do not attach to any retained chronic condition are dropped. The link step yields 84,334 fill–condition rows among 6,820 persons.

A single fill can link to more than one chronic ICD, which expands that fill into multiple rows after the CLNK merge. Summing RXDAYSUP on the expanded frame would double-count days of supply and, if only the first ICD were kept, would silently discard multi-condition context. The pipeline therefore deduplicates to one row per physical fill—(DUPERSID, DRUGIDX, RXRECIDX)—before any days-of-supply summation, reducing 84,334 expanded rows to 80,344 unique fills while preserving all 6,820 persons. Separately, a patient–drug–condition bridge is built with one row per (DUPERSID, DRUGIDX, ICD10CDX, CONDIDX) and no RXDAYSUP column, so condition-level rollups cannot accidentally sum supply. On the deduplicated fills, records are collapsed to one person–drug row by summing RXDAYSUP, taking the earliest start year and earliest valid start month (1–12), retaining first-encountered Multum classes and drug identifiers, and recording a primary ICD (the first chronic ICD encountered) together with n_chronic_conditions and a comma-joined list of all linked chronic ICDs. That collapse produces 21,718 person–drug pairs.

The cohort is then restricted to maintenance medications using the hand-labeled RXNAME list (1,158 unique names: 518 chronic, 373 flare-up, 267 non-chronic). Drugs labeled flare-up or non-chronic—acute courses such as antibiotics, steroid bursts, and selected topicals—are dropped so they do not contaminate chronic-condition adherence. After this filter, 16,461 person–drug pairs remain among 5,968 persons. Person-level demographics and participation fields from h251 are merged many-to-one on DUPERSID. Missing age (AGE23X equal to the MEPS sentinel −1) is recoded to the string “unknown.” Round-detail participation columns used only to compute the eligibility window are dropped from the final analytic frame after reference days are derived.

Eligible days in 2023 are not assumed to be a flat 365 for every respondent. Instead, each person’s coverage window is constructed from round-level person status (PSTATS) and reference begin and end dates (BEGRF/ENDRF) for rounds 3/1, 4/2, and 5/3, following the taxonomy documented in the h251 codebook (Tables 7–8). Persons with PSTATS equal to 11 in all three rounds are assigned a full-year window of 365 days from January 1 through December 31. For others, the algorithm walks the rounds in order: rounds with PSTATS of −1 (not in that round) are skipped; statuses that imply no survey coverage contribute no days; statuses that imply continued presence extend the coverage end date using the round’s ENDRF (clamped to the calendar year); death (status 31) and other stop statuses (moved out or otherwise ending participation) close the window at the round end and halt further extension. Unknown PSTATS codes are labeled UNCLASSIFIED rather than silently treated as active. The resulting person-level eligible-day count becomes the base denominator (total_days_supply). Persons who did not respond in Round 5/3 (PSTATS53 equal to −1)—137 persons in the 2023 person file—remain in the frame but are flagged: their denominator ends at the Round 4/2 interview date while dispensed days may still extend past that point, which can bias their adherence ratio upward.

A second, drug-specific adjustment shortens the denominator when a medication’s earliest fill in the year indicates a mid-year start. If the earliest RXBEGYRX equals 2023 and RXBEGMM is a valid month from 1 through 12, eligible days for that person–drug pair are set to the number of days from the first day of that month through December 31. Drugs that began before 2023, or whose start month is a sentinel or missing, keep the full person-year eligible window. Roughly 31.6 percent of 2023 chronic-drug pairs receive a shorter drug-start denominator; the remainder keep the person-level window (typically 365 for full-year respondents). The person–drug denominator used in the ratio is therefore the minimum of person eligible days and this drug-start window where applicable.

Adherence is computed in a PDC style with an explicit ceiling. For each person–drug pair, total_valid_days is the minimum of summed RXDAYSUP, 365, and the eligible-day denominator. The adherence ratio is one hundred times total_valid_days divided by the denominator when the denominator is positive, and missing when the denominator is zero. Capping the numerator prevents ratios above one hundred percent when overlapping or long supplies exceed the observation window; this is intentional PDC-style behavior rather than an uncapped medication possession ratio. Exploratory summaries in the project treat sixty percent as a display threshold for histograms and high-versus-low splits; that cutoff is an analytic convenience, not a clinical standard.

Taken together, the 2023 chronic-drug analytic sample contains 16,461 person–drug rows and 5,968 unique persons, starting from 192,275 raw fills. Several limitations should be kept in view when interpreting results. MEPS does not publish calendar fill dates; timing resolution is survey-round based (approximately four months), so true day-level PDC cannot be reconstructed. Households report fills with recall, and a substantial share of raw days-of-supply values are missing or coded Don’t Know and are therefore excluded. Condition coding is three-digit ICD-10 only, which collapses clinically distinct subtypes. The chronic ICD allowlist is a draft and should be treated cautiously until audited. The chronic-drug labels are hand-curated and define the maintenance cohort by design. Round 5/3 nonresponse can inflate adherence for a small flagged subgroup. Finally, the measure describes refill continuity in a survey sample; it does not support causal claims about clinical outcomes or intervention effects.
